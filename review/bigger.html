01 / THE PROBLEM

Same card. Five readings.

These are the actual card renders at each size. The only thing changing is the token. At 145 the rules are there, but they are not doing any work. At 200 they start to; at 220 they read.

The card is the 600 × 840 render scaled down; the widths below are the real table widths. The Index Tab stays 44px at every size.

145px rules decorative
180px name + stats
190px first rules line
200px rules readable
220px comfortable

Where the rules start

200px is the first size where a full rule sentence is readable without a lean. 190 is the compromise; 180 is an overview with better names and stats.

What you spend

Either window (height or width), hand placement (overlap or side shelf), or navigation chrome (focus mode). The lanes never get hidden.

05 / PICK

Choose the budget. Not the card.

The card size is one decision per table. The rest is what you are willing to spend: the nav, 52px of height, 100px of width, or a side hand.

MY PICK

B · 190px focus for the corner.
D · 200px bottom hand if it can grow.

B is the best same-window upgrade: 31% bigger cards, all three lanes, the Index Tab, and the bottom hand. The only sacrifice is the top nav — move the doors into the drawer. D is the first table where the rules genuinely read at rest; it costs a 1060×1012 window and a tighter score/dock.

  • Same window, keep nav: A · 180.
  • Same window, best read: B · 190 focus.
  • Window can grow: D · 200 bottom hand.
  • Biggest: E · 220 tall.
  • Layout frozen: F · detail lens.
