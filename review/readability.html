01 / DIAGNOSIS

The palette is not the problem. The distance is.

The screen is built like a desk at 1440 and then squeezed. It needs a small-window contract, a type floor, and an event voice that lands before the player has to look for it.

6px RULES TEXT

The card face whispers

Rules sit at 6–9px in the hand and board. At 480px they are below the readable threshold.

8px GUARD + PHASE

The state is a rumour

“1 GUARD · FACE SEALED”, the phase track, and the log all live at 8–10px.

⅓ OF THE WIDTH

Rails before cards

The player and intent rails take roughly a third of a 1440 table before a card gets a pixel.

164px DAMAGE PLATE

The hit is a receipt

A 36px number in a 164px plate is readable up close. At 480px it is 12px and easy to miss.

02 / FIVE ANSWERS

Not five font sizes. Five different rooms.

Each option chooses a different trade: less board for more card, less chrome for more number, or less table for more life. The same exchange runs in every frame: Cinder Hound strikes for 2,600, then KINDLE adds 400.

Frames render at actual CSS pixels. On a narrow pane, the 960 and 1100 frames scroll sideways rather than shrink.

03 / COMPARE

The same game, five distances.

Numbers are the contract, not the vibe. The reading floor counts names, rules, numbers, and buttons; decorative micro-tags sit one step below. The card width is the hero; the window is where the option stops working.
