S3 larger·a six-person design team

S3 is the floor.
Here is the ceiling.

S3 keeps the hand fully readable in one horizontal row, but that row costs a third card height: the board card is 209px at 1920×1080. A six-person team — tabletop UX, accessibility, stage design, information architecture, systems, and player psychology — sketched four ways to keep S3's one-line hand and give the board its missing height.

S3 · 209 → 292 same hand line · four new budgets
01 / THE BRIEF

The hand is the habit. The height is the problem.

S3 spends three card heights: two mats and a fully visible hand. The side hand spends two, but puts the hand in two columns. The team was told to keep the bottom hand as one line, keep every lane, keep one board token, and find the missing 100px without making the hand a hover-only puzzle.

S3 vertical budget 3 × card height + 202 chrome Side budget 2 × card height + 198 chrome Target keep one bottom hand line Never hide a lane or shrink a card inside the hand
02 / THE TEAM

Six fields. One table.

Each persona looked at the same S3 problem from a different discipline. Their sketches became the four options below; the vote is in the pick.

MV
Tabletop UX · card ergonomics

Mara Voss

“Move the chrome, not the hand.” A full hand is a habit; put the score and actions on the wall instead of taxing the table height.

IC
Accessibility · inclusive interaction

Ivo Chen

“A full hand at rest; pin, don't hover-only.” The peek shelf is allowed only if focus and click do the same job as hover.

SA
Stage & industrial design

Sana Adeyemi

“Let the shelf peek, then lift.” Treat the hand as a physical tray: a 30% lip at rest, a 160ms lift to the full card, a latched pin.

RC
Information architecture · typography

Rook Calder

“Names at rest, full face in a lens.” A 60px index of names and stats can buy the board a whole card height; the lens carries the face.

NP
Real-time systems · responsive budgets

Nadia Petrov

“Wide windows want a wider table.” On 16:9 and ultrawide, split the mats; the hand stays one row and the width does the work.

EP
Game systems · player psychology

Elias Park

“S3 is the map. Keep the map.” The winner should feel like S3 at a glance: same lane order, same hand row, same place for the eye to land.

VOTE Spring Shelf · 3 Wall Chrome · 2 Hand Index + Lens · 1 Open Table · wide-screen wildcard SHIP Spring Shelf, with Wall Chrome as the no-hover fallback

05 / PICK

S3, with a spring. Or S3 with walls.

The team's vote is not for the biggest card; it is for the option that still feels like the table the player already knows. That is the Spring Shelf. Wall Chrome is the no-hover fallback; Lens and Open Table are specialist answers.

TEAM PICK

Spring Shelf (X2).
S3's hand, 35% more board.

One bottom line. A 30% resting shelf with every name and type visible. Hover, focus, or click lifts the full card; click pins it. 283px at 1920, 225 at 1440, 399 at 2560, 256 at square. It is the only option that keeps S3's lane map and hand line while buying a real height budget.

  • X2 Spring Shelf: S3 feel; one line; +35%; reveal on hover/focus/click.
  • X1 Wall Chrome: full hand at rest, no hover; 245 at 1920; score/dock left.
  • X4 Hand Index + Lens: max board 292/421; tabs + full lens; one face at a time.
  • X3 Open Table: 354/450 on 2560/3440; full hand; wide only.
  • Never: hide a lane, shrink a card inside the hand, or make hover the only read.
