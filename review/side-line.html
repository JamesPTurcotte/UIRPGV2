Fluid side·one-line hand lab

The side hand.
One line, not two.

Fluid Side put the hand in two columns. These three versions keep every hand card on one line: a tight side fan, a side scroll, or a bottom row. The first two keep the maximum board card; the third keeps the hand fully readable and gives back board size.

01 / THE SECOND COLUMN

Two lines. One card width each.

The Fluid Side frame uses two hand columns. That fills the width (94–95%) but puts the hand on two lines. A single column uses one card width (4c + 226), keeps the same two-card-height board budget, and leaves the table group centered with wider side gutters. The card itself stays the same size in landscape — or grows at 1440 and square, where the two-column hand was width-limited.

02 / WHAT “ONE LINE” MEANS

One column or one row. Not two columns.

The side variants use a single vertical line on the right. The bottom variant uses a single horizontal row. All three keep the deck back as a full 5:7 card; the side fan dims it and labels the count so the deck reads as a stack, not as a stray face.

SIDE FAN

All six edges

One vertical line; older cards show a 20% strip, the newest card stays full. No scroll.

SIDE SCROLL

Three visible

One vertical line at 50% overlap; the remaining cards scroll. The readable side option.

BOTTOM ROW

All six cards

One horizontal line; the full hand is readable. The board returns to the bottom-hand size.

The same table. Three hand lines.

Each frame is actual size at the selected window. The side variants use the two-card-height board budget; the bottom variant uses the three-card-height budget. No lane is hidden.

What you get

All six cards on one line, no scroll, and the same maximum board card as the side layout. The card names are the visible index.

The cost

Only the older cards are 20% strips; the newest card is full. The table uses 77% of the width at 1920 and 78% at 2560; the rest is side gutter.

Hover or focus a strip to lift it to the full card. Click or tap pins the lift; click again to release. The newest card is already full.

The one horizontal line, all six cards readable, and no board cover. This is the safe one-line hand.

04 / EVERY WINDOW

The line wins the card. The row wins the hand.

The one-line side hand never gives up card size to the two-column layout; it matches it at 16:9 and gains at 1440 and square. The bottom row gives up height for a fully readable hand.

05 / PICK

One line. Pick the overflow.

The side line is the max-card one-line hand. The bottom row is the fully-readable one-line hand. Both are valid; they answer different worries.

RECOMMENDATION

Side scroll (S2)
or bottom row (S3).

S2 is the strongest one-line hand: one column, max board card, three readable cards and a scroll for the rest. S3 is the safest: all six cards on one horizontal line, no cover, at the cost of the board returning to 209 / 166 / 190. S1 is the no-scroll fan if seeing every card edge at once matters more than reading the full face.

  • S1 fan: one line, older cards 20%, newest full, no scroll.
  • S2 scroll: one line, three readable, scroll, max card.
  • S3 bottom: one line, all six readable, board 209/166/190.
  • C 2-col: still the width-filling layout at 94–95%; two lines.
  • Never: hide a lane or shrink a card inside the bottom row.
  • Drawer: left overlay for S1/S2; right overlay for S3.

This is a review page. The live table is still the fixed 190 / 1000 focus. S1/S2 keep the deck back as a full 5:7 card, dimmed and counted; S3 uses the normal bottom hand. The next port would choose one line and move the drawer to the matching side. If S3 is the favorite but still too small, the six-persona follow-up is S3 Larger →.
