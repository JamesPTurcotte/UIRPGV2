01 / WHAT THE COMMITTEE FOUND

The floor was fiction. The hit was invisible.

The first five options declared 10–11px floors while their card type sat at 6–9px. They also left the actual event model untouched. These are the four findings that changed the brief.

8px THE RULE CEILING

The card cannot speak

The largest table token is 150px with 8px rules. There is no code path to a 16px rule line at 1440.

0.04s THE 4× HIT

Speed deletes the event

A 0.95s damage plate becomes 40ms at 4×. Speed must compress travel, never the hold or the residue.

⅓ THE WIDTH TAX

Furniture before cards

Rails and gutters take ~34% of 1440. The card gets 39% of its lane and 6px rules.

5× THE SAME FACT

The guard is everywhere

One guard state appears in five places at three sizes. The screen is busy, not informative.

THE GEOMETRY

A 5:7 card with 16px rules needs ~230px. Two cards + a reading shelf + a hand strip is a 560–680px play surface. Below that, the cards must leave the surface.

THE VERB

Always-on-top means unfocused, off-axis, and glanced at. It is a different job from playing a duel. The first pass never chose a verb.

THE HIT

Damage needs a body, a destination, and residue. A bigger floating plate is still a receipt. The committee refused another receipt.

What changes

04 / DECISION

Pick a surface. Or pick the stack.

The committee's recommendation is not one layout. It is a family: **Vigil** for the corner, **Altar** for the playable small window, **Broadside** for full screen, with **Sealbreak** as the Warden and the hit language. If you want one, Altar is the answer.
